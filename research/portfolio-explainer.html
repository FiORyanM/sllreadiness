Carbon credit portfolio

Follow Sara — 60-person logistics firm in Doha, €10k to cover ~800t of 2025 deliveries. She fears overpaying in a December panic and being accused of greenwash. This doc answers her five decisions in order.

A carbon credit = 1 tonne of CO₂ reduced or removed elsewhere, issued with a serial number in a registry (Verra, Gold Standard, Global Carbon Council). Throughout: holding (transferable, re-sellable) vs retirement (permanently cancelled in Sara's company name — the only point a climate claim is valid).

Quality dispersion — what this means (expand)

Same name — “1 tonne” — very different contents, so very different prices:

  • ~$2 wind power: the plant would likely be built anyway (low additionality), so the claim feels weak.
  • ~$6 old forest protection (REDD): fell 23% in price after 2023 over-crediting findings; volumes halved.
  • ~$8 metered stoves with CCP stamp: metered fuel savings, independently checked — costs more because it can be proved.
  • ~$12–16 new trees (ARR) / forest management: removals with a story, but trees take years to grow.
  • ~$110 biochar / $300+ direct air capture: locked away 1,000+ years — hardest, most expensive.

Same unit, different provability, lock duration, year made (vintage), and rating (BeZero A vs C) — hence Carbonmark's $0.10–$650 range.

Hidden fees: a project may receive $5 while a broker resells the same tonne at $15 plus a 15.5% fee charged on the $15 — the buyer pays ~$17.30 for $5 of climate action. AlliedOffsets found ~250 projects resold at 3x or more. 90% of intermediaries disclose nothing, so a “cheap” $5 offer can hide a 400% margin. That is why every tonne in this product is split as project + $0.02 registry + $0.05 market + our disclosed 8–12%.

Claim risk. Product-level “carbon neutral” via offsets is banned in the EU; company claims require separated disclosure + third-party proof.Empowering Consumers Dir 2024/825 [6]; Green Claims proposal + Parliament/Council texts [7]; VCMI Claims Code v3 [8]; CSRD ESRS E1-7 [9].
Claim risk — what this means (expand)

Product level — banned. A chocolate bar labeled “carbon neutral chocolate” because offsets were bought elsewhere is always banned: shoppers read it as “this bar itself has no footprint,” which misleads, especially given over-estimation and double-counting risks in some credits.

Company level — allowed only if split open + proved by a third party:

  • Report own gross emissions and own cuts separately — never net credits against them.
  • Disclose offsets separately: tonnes retired vs contracted-future, share of residuals, reduction vs removal, standard, registry, EU share, and Article 6 corresponding-adjustment share.
  • Meet tier logic (e.g. Silver 10–<50%, Gold 50–<100%, Platinum ≥100%, rising yearly) without counting credits as internal cuts.
  • Prove each tonne: registry link with serial numbers, PDF certificate, plus independent verification — not self-declared.

Rule of thumb: products may not claim neutrality via offsets; companies may contribute via offsets only with two open columns — “what we cut” vs “what we bought” — every tonne traceable to a public retirement.

Chapter 1 · Sara decides what counts

1Mandate — what we capture

Sara answers five questions. Hard rules filter; wishes rank. She avoids the December scramble by locking rules in January.

What “locking rules in January” means (expand)

Year-end is retirement season — everyone closes books at once, good credits sell out first and prices spike on what is left. Locking rules in January does not mean buying everything in January; it means Sara fixes budget, trust bar, mix, dip threshold, monthly amount, and forward target up front, so the engine stages purchases through the year: cheap months buy more, expensive months buy less, quality lots get locked early. No December panic order of “800t of anything.”

How long carbon stays stored. Forest ~100y vs engineered 1000y+ [11].

Chapter 3 · Sara watches her account

3Holding + seeing value

Sara avoids opening registry accounts herself. She checks lots like a bank app through the year.

  • Where: alive lots in sub-accounts under our masters (Verra $750+$750/yr [17]; Gold $1,000/yr [18] — saved for you).
  • What you see per lot: quantity · average cost · current mark · vintage · registry · rating · CCP/CORSIA flags.
  • Marks (today's estimate): each lot shows an estimated price compiled daily from three public price reporters (Platts, OPIS, Argus [19]) plus live prints on the CBL exchange for that type. If a lot barely trades, it gets a thin-liquidity badge with a price range instead of a single price — never shown as a stock tick, because ⅔ of deals are private with no single benchmark (IOSCO warning [20]).
Single-type bets are fragile — 2024 moved types in opposite directions: REDD+ −23% price on −52% volume while waste and IFM volumes surged [1].

Chapter 4 · Sara's year

4Buying cheaper without betting

An illustrative walk-through of Sara's year — months and prices are simplified to show how the three levers sequence. Every scene ends as a draft.

Illustrative scenario · Sara · €10k / ~800t
  • MARDip ping — liked forest type drops 12% vs its 30-day average → we ping “buy 100t now to save ~$100?” → Sara approves or ignores. No auto-charge.
  • JUN–AUGMonthly buys — €800/month auto-drafted; cheap months convert to more tonnes, expensive months to fewer. Smooths the price without timing the market.
  • OCTForward lock for 2028 — she signs for 50t of 2028 biochar at today's $160/t → deposit now, delivery in 2028. Locks supply; waits years.
Why this sequence — our reasoning (expand)

This order is proposed by our execution plan, not chosen by Sara month by month. Three layers:

  • This year first, future second. The 2025 ~800t is must-cover (retire and report); 2028 forwards are upside. The engine secures near-term coverage before allocating leftover budget to long-dated locks — never the reverse.
  • Cash and information clear later. January demand is an estimate; actuals sharpen by autumn. Forwards need deposits, due diligence, and negotiation, so they wait until remaining volume and budget are known. Monthly averaging plus opportunistic dips carry the first half without market timing.
  • Recomputed after every execution. Each dip buy and monthly draft updates remaining volume and budget; the forward suggestion equals the minimum of forecast gap, budget left, and concentration caps. Sara always sees advice based on what already happened — never a script frozen in January.
  • Dip alerts — e.g. A-rated forest −10% vs 30-day avg → “buy 100t to save ~$X?” [19]
  • Monthly averaging — fixed cash/month; high months buy less, low months buy more.
  • Forward locks (offtakes) — contract today for future delivery (e.g. 50t of 2028 biochar at $160/t [21]). Supplier finances construction; you lock supply. Years out, not spot — Microsoft 45Mt FY25 + Frontier $317M model [22].

Same $1,000 buys 91t at $11 vs 167t at $6 — avoids December panic buying.

Separation of dutiesWe do not run registries. Verra, Gold Standard, and GCC Doha do. We run mandate, mix, ledger, valuation, and proof on top — same pattern as the existing ESG tool (uploads → job queues → scored reports → PDF export).

Mandate → mix

  • Filter out — old vintages (pre-2016 default), ICVCM-rejected methods, below-minimum ratings.
  • Score — four checks, each 0–100, weighted into one rating (還沒跟我們原本的framework結合）:
    • Provability — can the tonne be proved? Measured with meters/satellites (not models alone) + independently verified + serial-tracked. How we check: methodology version, verifier report, registry issuance records, BeZero/Sylvera rating.
    • Lock duration (permanence) — how long the carbon stays stored, and what backs reversals (e.g. forest buffer pool vs geological storage). How we check: project type + commitment period + buffer/insurance terms.
    • Would-it-happen-anyway (additionality) — would this cleanup exist without credit money? Wind farms near cost parity score low; metered stoves replacing firewood score higher. How we check: financial/regulatory tests in the methodology + BCG/Shell parity evidence.
    • Resale liquidity — could we exit before retiring? Recent prints on CBL, bid depth, OTC desk quotes for that type/vintage. How we check: 30-day prints + volume tiers; thin lots get the badge instead of a single price.
  • Allocate — Budget / Balanced / Future-proof within caps + budget / removal / stamp checks.
Inline Qatar example — step by step
  • 1. Sara ticks “prefer Qatar.” The engine searches Doha-issued GCC credits first — Baladna composting, Al-Shaheen gas recovery.
  • 2. She asks for 100% Qatar. The engine refuses to build it silently: Qatar supply is only 2–3 types and trades over-the-counter (not on the live exchange), so a pure-Qatar bag trips two badges — concentration (too few projects) + thin liquidity (hard to resell).
  • 3. Engine proposes an alternative. Qatar-anchored: 30–50% Qatar for the story + MENA/global balance for safety — with the cost/rating trade-off shown side by side. Sara approves or adjusts.

Custody + ledger

  • Alive (hold/sell) → retired (dead, proof issued) or transferred. Each lot tracks project, registry, year, serials, cost, rating, stamps.
  • Pre-funded wallet for same-day moves; large deals swap money + credits together. Daily reconcile; registry is final.

Valuation + execution

  • Daily pull of public assessments + live prints, each stamped with source, date, liquidity badge.
  • Dips (~10% vs 30-day), monthly schedules (capped, pausable), forwards (deposit/milestone + failure buffer) all create drafts — no auto-charge.

Retire + disclose

  • Two-phase confirm because retirement is irreversible: draft → retype legal name to confirm.
  • Registry call executes; serials + certificate stored; disclosure pack builds (tier math, splits, audit export). Banned wording blocked here.

Appendix C · Strategy

Why save-cost first, trade parked

Save-cost worksUse held at 182Mt while trading fell to 84Mt [1]. Averaging + dips + forwards cut 10–20% vs December panic. No exit needed. Fits 93% with strategy (avg $5.4M/yr [28]).
Trade usually fails⅔ private deals [20]; $0.10 fees erase $0.30 credits; basket $18.10 → $0.77 in a month; hidden 15.5%+ and 3x resales on 250 projects [5]. Compliance futures (KRBN +147% [29]) are regulated + volatile — gated, not V1.
